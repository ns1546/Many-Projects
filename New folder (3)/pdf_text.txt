0.1. Introduction
Air pollution is one of the most critical environmental and public health challenges
facing Bangladesh today. According to theIQAir World Air Quality Report
2023, Dhaka consistently ranks among thetop three most polluted capital
cities in the world, with an annual average PM2.5 concentration far exceeding the
World Health Organization (WHO) safe limit of 5 µg/m3. The health consequences
of prolonged exposure to poor air quality are severe and well-documented, including
respiratory diseases, cardiovascular disorders, lung cancer, and premature death.
Despite the gravity of this problem, the vast majority of people in Bangladesh
haveno access to real-time, localized air quality information. The few
government monitoring stations that exist are limited in number and geographic
coverage, and none providepredictive warningsbefore air quality deteriorates
to dangerous levels. People are routinely exposed to hazardous air without any
knowledge or warning.
This project proposes aSmart Air Quality Monitoring and Health Risk
Prediction Systemthat addresses this critical gap. The system uses anESP32
microcontroller, multiple environmental and gas sensors, aMachine Learning
(ML)prediction engine, and anIoT cloud platformto continuously monitor
air quality, calculate a real-timeAir Quality Index (AQI)score, predict future
health risk, and provide immediate alerts through multiple channels.
Key Motivation
Dhakais ranked among the world’s most polluted cities. This affordable
system (under4,500) provides real-time monitoring andpredictivehealth
risk warnings, giving people time to act BEFORE air becomes dangerous — a
capability that no existing affordable system currently provides in Bangladesh.
0.2. Problem Statement
Core Problems Identified
Problem 1: No Localized Real-Time Monitoring Government AQI
stations are limited to a few locations in major cities. Air quality varies
significantly between neighborhoods, buildings, and even individual rooms.
No affordable device exists for individuals or institutions to monitor their
immediate environment.
Department of Computer Science and Engineering•BUBT 2Smart Air Quality Monitoring & Health Risk Prediction System Project Proposal
Problem 2: Reactive Systems Only All existing low-cost systems only
measurecurrentconditions. They alert usersafterthe air has already
become dangerous. No system warns usersbeforeair quality deteriorates.
Problem 3: Single Pollutant Detection Simple sensor systems typically
detect only one gas. True health risk depends on the combined effect of
multiple pollutants: CO 2, CO, PM2.5, temperature, and humidity together
determine real danger levels.
Problem 4: No Health Guidance for Common People Raw sensor
data such as ADC values and ppm numbers is meaningless to ordinary
users. A clear, standardized, and actionable health status system is needed.
Problem 5: High Cost of Commercial Solutions Commercial air
quality monitors with prediction capability cost 10,000 to 30,000+,
making them completely unaffordable for most Bangladeshi households
and institutions.

0.6.3 System Working Flow
Step-by-Step System Operation
Step 1: Sensor Reading (Every 2 seconds) ESP32 reads all four sen-
sors simultaneously: MQ-135 provides CO 2/gas value, MQ-7 provides CO
gas value, GP2Y1010 provides dust density in µg/m3, and DHT22 provides
temperature in°C and relative humidity in %.
Step 2: AQI Calculation ESP32 converts each sensor reading to an AQI
sub-index using the US EPA formula. The overall AQI score is the
maximum of all sub-indices. Health level is determined: Good, Moderate,
Unhealthy, or Hazardous.
Step 3: ML Prediction Linear Regression predicts the AQI value for the
next one hour. Decision Tree classifies the predicted future health risk
level. AQI trend analysis determines if air quality is rising, falling, or
stable.
Step 4: Local Output Update OLED display shows three rotating infor-
Department of Computer Science and Engineering•BUBT 8Smart Air Quality Monitoring & Health Risk Prediction System Project Proposal
mation pages. RGB LED changes color to match current health level.
Buzzer sounds if health level reaches Unhealthy or Hazardous.
Step 5: Cloud Upload (Every 15 seconds) ESP32 connects to WiFi
and sends an HTTP request to ThingSpeak API with 8 data fields including
all sensor values, AQI score, and ML predictions.
Step 6: Web Dashboard Update JavaScript on the web dashboard
fetches ThingSpeak data every 15 seconds and updates all gauges, charts,
and health advice panels automatically in the user’s browser.
