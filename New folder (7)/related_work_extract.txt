Project Title : Multilingual (English and Bangla) Spam Detection Using NLP and Machine Learning. 
 
Overview 
 
While digital communication systems evolve, the malicious behavior toward such communication means 
by the use of various kinds of cyberattacks (spam, smishing, phishing) has unfortunately grown. Thus, a 
considerable amount of research has been done in the fields of machine learning, deep learning, and natural 
language processing techniques. Starting from work on messaging systems, Almeida et al. [1] proposed the 
publicly available Public SMS Spam Collection dataset. Various machine learning methods were 
considered; it was discovered that Support Vector Machine (SVM) showed outstanding initial results and 
reached 97.64% accuracy. Nevertheless, due to the brevity of SMS text, the variety of features and message 
semantic understanding was severely restricted. In terms of email spam recognition, Fattahi et al. [9] created 
"SpamDL" using GloVe vectorization and Bi-LSTM models. Though deep learning models proved to be 
highly effective in recognizing patterns, they can be efficiently used only on emails, not informal messages 
found in mobile phones. 
 
There have also been approaches for identifying suspicious web links. Gupta et al. [2] created a hybrid 
classifier that leveraged the attributes generated from the URL strings and the DOM structure of the 
webpage and achieved 99.17% accuracy using XGBoost. The major weakness with this method was its 
vulnerability to being evaded through embedded objects or any form of page which isn’t HTML. Another 
approach was taken by Dutta [3], who based his work on identifying suspicious URL strings by using only 
RNN-LSTM networks. He attained a high accuracy rate in dealing with zero-day attacks (97.4%) but faced 
computational complexity and the inadequacy of unsupervised feature extraction techniques. On the other 
hand, Kumar et al. [8] used heuristics generated from source codes and URLs to identify suspicious URLs 
and used machine learning models like Random Forest. They were able to attain a high 91.4% accuracy 
rate, but they found out that there was a need for further validation of the model. 
 
To deal with the changing strategies of attackers through mobile channels, several researchers have 
considered text normalization and behavioral factors. According to Goel et al. [5], a dedicated slang 
dictionary was used for transforming abbreviations to text before classifying using the Naïve Bayes 
technique, and thus achieving a 96.2% detection rate. However, in their study, they did not consider the 
behavior of the URLs contained in the text at all. In contrast, "SmishGuard" by Samad et al. [6] combined 
the techniques of TF-IDF, Latent Dirichlet Allocation, and Word2Vec to attain a detection accuracy of 
98.47%. Later, Hosseinpour and Das [10] introduced a multi-signal method that integrates semantic 
labeling and structure of messages for detecting evasive smishing, obtaining 97.89% accuracy on a massive 
scale of data. The one disadvantage in such highly complex systems is the extensive dependency of these 
models on English-based corpora. 
 
To bridge the gap for resource-scarce languages, especially those involving speakers in South Asia, there 
is a dire need for highly customized techniques. Alam et al. [4] considered fine-tuning multilingual 
transformer architectures for text classifications of Bangla, with reasonable success but still recognizing the 
lack of a good mono-language transformer in Bangla for handling the intricacies of local dialects. A 

commendable step was taken by Talukder et al. [7], who came up with a multi-class deep learning system 
using BiLSTM architecture to classify Bangla messages as ham, smishing, phishing, and financial frauds, 
with an overall success rate of 99%. Though highly impressive, there is no mention of the NLP-specific 
customization needed to detect localized new financial scams such as bKash, Nagad, and lottery scams, 
which are often written in a mixture of Bangla, English, and phonetic spelling.
