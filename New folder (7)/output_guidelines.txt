Basic Instructions 
• This guideline focuses on writing technical papers, particularly those related to 
artificial intelligence. 
• A well-written journal paper should be between 15 and 25 pages of standard 
formatting. 
• Plagiarism must be kept below 15%, with no single source contributing more than 
1% of similarity. 
• Formal language is required. The paper should use clear and precise language that 
is accessible to a broad audience. 
• Minimize the use of AI-generated writing as much as possible. 
• Start writing, before thinking.

Structure of a Paper
Various chapters appear in different types of papers. Topics are not limited. We 
suggest including the following chapters.
1. Abstract
2. Introduction / Overview
3. Related Works / Literature Review/ Background Study
4. Methodology/ Approach/ Design
5. Result and Discussion / Analysis
6. Conclusion 
7. References

Formulating the Title of Your Article
The title of your research is the very first point of contact for readers, reviewers, 
and search engines. Here's how to ensure your title is impactful:
• Ensure clarity and brevity: The title should be informative, directly aligned with 
the research question, and free from unnecessary length or fluff.
• Avoid overused adjectives and generic phrases: Words like “novel,” “robust,” or 
“state-of-the-art” should only be used when the contribution is genuinely 
groundbreaking. Otherwise, they may come off as promotional.
• Keep it simple and meaningful: Avoid jargon or abbreviations unless they are 
universally known.
• Author section should be clean: Do not include honorifics or degrees such as 
“Dr.,” “Prof.,” or “Ph.D.” This information will appear elsewhere.
• Use relevant and specific keywords: Select 4–5 keywords that truly reflect the 
essence of your work and make it easier to index and retrieve.
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 8

Examples of Effective Titles
1. Clear and Targeted Titles
• “Deep Learning Models for Early Alzheimer’s Detection Using MRI Scans”
→ Immediately informs readers of the method (deep learning), goal (early detection), and 
domain (medical imaging).
• “Enhancing Self-Driving Vehicle Navigation through Reinforcement Learning”
→ Straightforward and tells the audience about a specific application and technique.
2. Engaging Titles that Spark Interest
• “Transforming Healthcare Outcomes Using AI: Predictive Insights through Machine Learning”
→ Strong verbs like “transforming” and application-focused wording attract attention.
• “Harnessing Artificial Intelligence to Revolutionize Supply Chain Optimization”
→ Bold and engaging, shows impact and relevance.
3. Concise and Keyword-Oriented
• “AI-Based Predictive Analytics for Detecting Fraud in Financial Transactions”
→ Includes strong search terms such as “AI,” “fraud detection,” and “predictive analytics.”

Examples of Effective Titles (continued)
4. Deep Learning Application Focus
• “Progress in Deep Learning for Real-Time Image Classification in Autonomous Driving”
→ Communicates method and real-time application.
• “Using CNNs to Identify Early Signs of Cancer from Radiological Images”
→ Clear use of convolutional networks in a life-critical domain.
5. Algorithm-Oriented Titles
• “Boosting Market Forecast Accuracy via Ensemble Machine Learning Models”
→ Suggests method (ensemble models) and domain (financial markets).
• “Reinforcement Learning Techniques to Minimize Energy Usage in Smart Grids”
→ Domain-specific and technically descriptive.
6. Leveraging Pretrained Models
• “Enhancing Image-Based Medical Diagnostics with Fine-Tuned CNN Architectures”
→ Indicates methodology (fine-tuning CNNs) and domain.
• “Sentiment Analysis on Social Media Using Pretrained Transformers”
→ A great fit for NLP applications involving BERT, GPT, etc.

Writing the Abstract
The abstract is a miniature version of your paper. It provides a quick snapshot of 
the entire research project, enabling readers to decide whether the paper is 
worth reading. A well-crafted abstract should include:
• Research Area and Significance: Why is this topic important?
• Problem Statement and Gap: What issue are you addressing that hasn’t been 
fully resolved?
• Objective: What does your work aim to achieve?
• Method Overview: What approach or techniques were used?
• Key Findings: What are the primary results or discoveries?
• Broader Implications: Why do these results matter, and what could be their 
impact?
Keep the abstract between 150–200 words. Make every sentence count, each 
should convey critical information in a clear and concise manner.

Writing a Strong Introduction
A powerful introduction hooks the reader, sets the research in context, and builds 
up logically to the central problem. Here’s how to do it effectively:
Step 1: Open with a Broad Context
Begin with an overview of the general domain (e.g., AI, healthcare, sustainability) 
and explain its relevance.
Example: “Artificial Intelligence has rapidly evolved over recent years, influencing 
a broad spectrum of industries from finance to autonomous systems.”
Step 2: Narrow Down the Problem
Zoom into the specific issue that your research tackles.
Example: “Despite significant progress in deep learning, existing models still 
struggle when applied to domains with limited labeled data.”
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 13

Writing a Strong Introduction (continued)
Step 3: State Your Research Objectives
Clearly outline what your study intends to achieve and how.
Example: “This paper explores transfer learning as a means of improving 
diagnostic accuracy in under-resourced healthcare environments.”
Step 4: Emphasize Significance
Explain why your work matters and who it benefits.
Example: “By adapting existing models to data-scarce settings, we aim to make AI 
solutions more accessible in rural medical clinics.”
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 14

Writing a Strong Introduction (continued)
Step 5: Briefly Describe the Method
Offer a one-sentence summary of your approach.
Example: “We fine-tuned ResNet-50 using medical X-ray data and compared its 
performance with a traditional CNN model.”
Step 6: Outline the Paper
• Provide a roadmap of what follows in the paper.
Example: “Section 2 surveys related work, Section 3 outlines the methodology, 
Section 4 presents results, and Section 5 discusses implications.”

Conducting a Literature Review
An effective literature review demonstrates your familiarity with the field and 
highlights gaps that your work addresses.
Key Steps:
1. Define the Scope:
Focus only on literature that supports your study’s goals. For example, if you're 
working on deep learning in healthcare, avoid unrelated AI literature.
2. Search Extensively:
Use scholarly databases like Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, PubMed. Prioritize recent 
papers and well-cited reviews.
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 16

Conducting a Literature Review
3. Categorize by Themes:
Group literature into themes such as “Transfer Learning Applications,” “Low-
Resource Environments,” or “Pretrained CNNs in Healthcare.”
4. Summarize and Synthesize:
Discuss trends, consistencies, and contradictions in the findings. Highlight what 
others have done and where their work falls short.
5. Highlight Gaps:
Show what hasn’t been explored or where methodologies fall short. This justifies 
the need for your study.
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 17

Conducting a Literature Review
6. Discuss Common Methods:
Note what tools, datasets, and evaluation techniques are commonly used. Mention 
weaknesses in these approaches if relevant.
7. End with Your Research Gap:
Conclude the section by stating what specific issue your study is designed to solve.

Methodology Section
The methodology section should be precise and transparent so that others can 
replicate your work. It should include:
1. Research Design: 
Describe whether it’s experimental, descriptive, or analytical.
Example: “This is a quantitative study evaluating the performance of CNN models 
on X-ray image classification.”
2. Data Collection: 
Mention dataset source, size, and how data was cleaned or preprocessed. 
Example: “We used 5,000 images from ChestX-ray14, resized to 224x224 for model 
compatibility.”
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 19

Methodology Section (continued)
3. Data Preprocessing: 
Describe how raw data was prepared for model input, including normalization, 
encoding, augmentation, or splitting.
4. Experimental Procedures: 
Detail the algorithms, training processes, and performance evaluation. Include 
hyperparameters like learning rate, epochs, batch size, etc.
5. Evaluation Metrics: 
Mention which metrics were used (accuracy, F1-score, AUC, etc.) and how 
performance was statistically validated.
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 20

Methodology Section (continued)
6. Justification for Choices: 
Explain why certain models or frameworks were used.
Example: “ResNet50 was selected for its robustness in image recognition tasks.”
7. Ethical Considerations: 
Address how privacy and ethical issues were managed, particularly with human 
data.
8. Tools and Libraries Used: 
Example: “We used Python with Keras and TensorFlow, leveraging OpenCV and 
NumPy for data preprocessing.”

Representing and Analyzing Results
1.  Highlight Key Findings First:
Start with the headline results—did your approach work? By how much did it 
outperform the baseline?
2.  Use Tables and Visuals:
Support findings with comparison tables, accuracy curves, confusion matrices, 
etc.
3.  Report All Metrics:
Include precision, recall, F1, AUC, standard deviation, p-values—whatever is 
relevant to support statistical significance.
8/23/2025 Peer Research Lab 24

Representing and Analyzing Results (continued)
4. Discuss Statistical Tests:
If you did hypothesis testing (e.g., t-tests), report values clearly and state what 
they imply.
5. Compare Methods:
Benchmark your model against existing solutions.
Example: “ResNet50 achieved 87.5% accuracy, outperforming VGG16 and 
baseline CNNs.”
6. Stay Neutral and Objective:
Let the results speak for themselves. Avoid exaggerations and remain factual.

Referencing in article
• Cite to credit sources & avoid plagiarism
• Use IEEE, APA, MLA, or Chicago as required
• Tools: Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote, BibTeX
• Prefer recent, peer-reviewed, and original works
• Double-check citation accuracy & formatting
